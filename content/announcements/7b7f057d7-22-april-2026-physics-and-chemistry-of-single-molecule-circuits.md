# Abstract

The past decade has seen tremendous progress in realizing molecular
analogues of macroscale electronic components, such as resistors,
diodes, switches or transistors, where the molecular circuit property is
determined by the chemical structure and physical properties of the
metal-molecule-metal junction. This progress has been enabled by the
scanning tunneling microscope (STM)-based break-junction technique that
my group has pioneered, which facilitates reliable and reproducible
measurements of single-molecule devices. In this talk, I will first
describe how we developed this technique to probe fundamental properties
of circuit elements created with organic molecules and then give you an
example of how we created long and highly conducting molecular wires
using one-dimensional molecular analogs of topological insulators.
