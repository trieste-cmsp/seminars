# Abstract

Kagome metals have emerged as a fascinating platform for exploring the interplay between lattice geometry, electronic correlations, and band topology. Their distinctive electronic structure, characterized by Dirac fermions, flat bands, and van Hove singularities, provides an ideal setting for unconventional electronic phases and spontaneous symmetry breaking. In this talk, I will discuss how the peculiar electronic structure of kagome metals gives rise to a rich landscape of quantum phenomena, focusing on insights gained from the combination of first-principles calculations, many-body approaches, and angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy. I will first highlight the role of multiple van Hove singularities and their sublattice character in shaping electronic instabilities, including charge order and unconventional superconductivity in the AV3Sb5 family. I will then turn to bilayer kagome metals, where the interplay between flat bands and spin-orbit coupling generates no ntrivial spin Berry curvature, providing a connection between lattice geometry and topological electronic responses. Finally, I will discuss recent advances in understanding electronic nematicity in CsTi3Bi5, where orbital-selective correlations drive a Pomeranchuk instability and spontaneous rotational symmetry breaking. Together, these results illustrate how the kagome lattice provides a versatile framework for investigating the emergence of collective electronic phenomena and identifying new routes toward controlling topology and correlations in quantum materials.
